## 1. Family A — what it is: agent-plus-framework combinations in real use, viral examples, skills/starter kits, step-by-step basics

### Takeaway
"Prompt a coding agent, get an MP4" is mainstream by November 2026: Remotion says Agent Skills have "become the default way people make videos with Remotion", HeyGen's Apache-licensed HyperFrames has 85.5k GitHub stars about seven months after being open-sourced, and one curated list tracks 515 agent-video skill repositories. Claude Code and Codex are the best-supported agents; setup is about six terminal commands for Remotion, or no terminal at all with HyperFrames' free Studio desktop app.

### Inferences
- Ranking by evidence of real use in late 2026: (1) Claude Code or Codex + Remotion; (2) any skills-capable agent + HyperFrames; (3) agent + Manim for maths/concept explainers; (4) Three.js / React Three Fiber scenes inside Remotion or HyperFrames for 3D shots; (5) agent + Blender MCP for true 3D. Motion Canvas should not be chosen for new work (no release since 2024).
- Gemini CLI's old free tier is no longer a reason to choose it; Google's free access now sits in a different product (Antigravity CLI).
- The ecosystem has moved from "one skill" (Jan 2026) to opinionated production kits with storyboards, recipe cards, QA and critic loops, which suggests raw one-prompt output was not good enough on its own.

## 2. Family A — what it can and cannot do; how professional it looks

### Takeaway
Code-driven video suits anything that is designed rather than filmed: kinetic typography, product and UI showcases built from real screenshots, 3D device mockups, logo and 3D-text stings, charts, maps, code animations and maths explainers — all deterministic, editable and text-sharp. It does not produce photoreal footage or character performance, and its finish depends on the model's design sense and on iteration: the vendors themselves say top models are "still better at the hard parts: timing, composition, finish".

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** Content types Remotion handles natively (and that use the GPU): "WebGL content (Three.js, Skia, P5.js, Mapbox, etc.)", video, shadows, gradients, blur, transforms, 2D canvas — [Remotion GPU doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/gpu.mdx)
- **[P]** Remotion's skills cover map animations ("animated routes and markers, Mapbox, MapLibre, MapTiler, GeoJSON, and CesiumJS flyovers") and captions — [Remotion skills doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/ai/skills.mdx)
- **[P]** 3D device mockups are a first-party use case: the React Three Fiber template is a 3D phone with a video on its screen — [template-three](https://github.com/remotion-dev/template-three)
- **[P]** HyperFrames supports Three.js as a built-in seekable runtime (also GSAP, CSS, Anime.js, Lottie, Web Animations, TypeGPU), has a WebGL shader-transitions package, and ships a `path-traced-3d` example for shots needing "soft shadows, light bouncing between objects and reflections" — [frame adapters](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/concepts/frame-adapters.mdx); [path-tracing guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/path-tracing.mdx); [repo](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)
- **[P]** Product videos are built from real captures, not generated pixels: HyperFrames says screens are "captured from the real site, not mocked"; video-shotcraft says "It is not a video generator. Every frame is drawn by code from your own screenshots and copy" and offers "exploded-view" and "2.5D page camera" shots — [product-launch-video guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/product-launch-video.mdx); [video-shotcraft](https://github.com/Vincentwei1021/video-shotcraft)
- **[P]** Explainers: anything2explainer makes narrated motion-graphics explainers with "No stock footage, no generative video model"; output is 1280×720 16:9 only ("Does it do vertical (9:16) video? Not currently") — [anything2explainer](https://github.com/Vincentwei1021/anything2explainer)
- **[P]** Data visualisation exists but is thinly served: the curated list shows a CSV-to-animated-chart skill with 19 stars and HyperFrames chart templates with 5 stars, plus data-viz skills inside the 1.7k-star motion-skills pack — [awesome-claude-video-skills](https://github.com/zhuyansen/awesome-claude-video-skills)
- **[P]** Character and photoreal work needs other pipelines even inside these kits: claude-code-video-toolkit treats 3D character films as an optional Blender add-on that "renders in minutes with a GPU" and uses separate AI models for talking heads and video; OpenMontage mentions Blender only for one showcase "Rendered with Blender Eevee/Cycles"; a separate 444-star project turns video into Mixamo-rig animation applied in Blender via MCP — [claude-code-video-toolkit](https://github.com/digitalsamba/claude-code-video-toolkit); [OpenMontage](https://github.com/OpenMontageInc/OpenMontage); [awesome-claude-video-skills](https://github.com/zhuyansen/awesome-claude-video-skills)
- **[P]** Quality depends on the model (vendor claim): in HeyGen's Code2Video benchmark (168 briefs written with the HyperFrames skills), four open-weight models "all landed within 50 Elo points of first place", but "The top models are still better at the hard parts: timing, composition, finish"; "For a first draft, a social post or learning the tool, an open model is often enough" — [HyperFrames free-agents guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/free-agents.mdx)
- **[P]** Signals that default output looks generic: popular add-on skills are pitched as making "AI-slop-style videos look like motion-designer work" (motionmaxxing, 1.6k stars) or add "a critic loop" (motion-video-kit, 1.4k stars) — [awesome-claude-video-skills](https://github.com/zhuyansen/awesome-claude-video-skills)
- **[P]** Results are reproducible and independent of playback speed: HyperFrames promises "same input, same frames, same output" and requests each exact frame "so a slow machine should not drop frames from the final video" — [HyperFrames repo](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes); [introduction](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/introduction.mdx)
- **[P]** After Effects assets can be reused: Remotion documents converting AE compositions via Bodymovin/Lottie (`@remotion/lottie`) — [Remotion After Effects doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/after-effects.mdx)
- **[P]** Maturity (vendor comparison written by HyperFrames): "Remotion is older and much more established", with more templates, tutorials and production history — [HyperFrames vs Remotion](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/hyperframes-vs-remotion.mdx)
- **[S]** Creator-reported iteration cost: a launch video took "~100 prompts, not 1" — [Demo Editor](https://demoeditor.com/articles/remotion-claude-code-launch-video)

### Inferences
- Good fits for "3D-looking" social content: device mockups with screen recordings, extruded logo/3D text stings, camera moves across layered UI (2.5D), exploded views, simple product turntables from a GLB model, abstract Three.js/shader backgrounds, 3D charts and globe/map flyovers.
- Poor fits: character animation, faces, photoreal environments, fluid/cloth/organic motion, anything whose appeal is "cinematic footage". Blender through an agent can in principle do these, but the documented need to split complex requests and the GPU dependence make it the weakest option for this user.
- Versus AI video generators: Family A wins on legible text, brand accuracy, exact timing, repeatability and near-zero cost per revision; it loses on realism and "wow" imagery. Versus a skilled After Effects/Blender artist: it reaches the level of a clean SaaS launch video or explainer; the ceiling is lower unless the creator supplies references, storyboards and several review passes.

- No independent blind comparison of agent-made motion graphics against professional After Effects/Blender work was reachable.

## 3. Family A — hardware: CPU-only / integrated-graphics rendering, render times, cloud rendering

### Takeaway
Yes, it runs on a weak PC: these tools render offline, frame by frame, in headless Chrome (Manim uses the CPU-based Cairo renderer) and encode on the CPU, so a slow machine only means a longer wait. Ordinary 2D/DOM motion graphics are light; WebGL/Three.js shots are the slow case when Chrome falls back to software rendering, and path tracing is impractical without a real GPU. I found no trustworthy benchmark on a weak PC, so the minutes-not-hours estimate below is an inference. Cloud rendering is cheap: cents per clip on Remotion Lambda, and HyperFrames' hosted renderer includes 5 exports a month free.

### Cited Findings

**Local rendering without a dedicated GPU**
- **[P]** Remotion: "in headless mode, Chromium disables the GPU, leading to a significant slowdown in rendering time"; the `--gl` flag re-enables it; "AWS Lambda instances have no GPU, so it is not possible to use it" (i.e. Lambda renders the same content in software) — [Remotion GPU doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/gpu.mdx)
- **[P]** Remotion `--gl` accepts `angle`, `egl`, `swiftshader`, `swangle`, `vulkan`, `angle-egl`; the default is to let Chrome decide, and on Lambda/Cloud Run it is `swangle` (software); `angle` was dropped as default because of "a small memory leak that could crash long Remotion renders" — [Remotion gl option](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/options/gl.mdx)
- **[S]** Remotion's GL page: "On a machine with no GPU, swangle is recommended. Rendering might be slow", and you "can still render all types of content without having a GPU, it will just be slower" — [Remotion gl-options (site)](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options)
- **[S]** Unofficial figures from a Chinese-language docs mirror put software rendering at roughly 3–5x (swangle) to 4–8x (SwiftShader) slower than GPU-accelerated ANGLE; not confirmed by official docs — [docs mirror](https://remotionlab.pages.dev/docs/misc-gl-flag)
- **[P]** Remotion performance guidance: GPU-dependent effects (WebGL, canvas, shadows, gradients, blur) "may take a long time to render" on GPU-less instances; tune with `--concurrency` and `npx remotion benchmark`; "Higher resolutions will make the render slower", use `--scale`; PNG frames are slower than JPEG; WebM codecs "are very slow at encoding" — [Remotion performance doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/performance.mdx)
- **[P]** Remotion encodes in software by default: the hardware-acceleration option defaults to `disable` — [hardware-acceleration option](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/options/hardware-acceleration.mdx)
- **[P]** Remotion can also render in the visible browser (client-side rendering): `<ThreeCanvas>` is supported, and "in the headful browser, GPU is usually available, which brings performance benefits"; limits include no multithreading, WebCodecs required, and many unsupported CSS features — [client-side rendering limitations](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/client-side-rendering/limitations.mdx)
- **[P]** HyperFrames local rendering "starts quickly and can use the computer's browser GPU" (but "On WSL2, Chrome may not reach the GPU"); defaults are 1920×1080 at 30 fps; 4K is "roughly 4× slower per frame"; `--fps 60` "creates more frames, so rendering takes longer" — [HyperFrames rendering guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/rendering.mdx)

- **[P]** Only published speed figures (machine not stated, vendor measurement): "a 300-frame 1920×1080 composition rendered in 9.8 seconds"; the same 300 frames took 25.0 s before one CSS change. ### Inferences
- Rough render-time estimate for a 15–30 s 1080p30 clip (450–900 frames) on a 4-core laptop CPU with integrated graphics — an estimate, not a measurement: HyperFrames' published 12–30 frames per second is from an unnamed, probably fast machine; assuming a weak PC is 3–6x slower gives about 2–10 minutes for DOM/CSS/SVG motion graphics. Heavy WebGL forced through software GL could be several times slower again (tens of minutes); path-traced 3D is not realistic locally.
- Integrated graphics is not the same as "no GPU": Chrome can use an Intel/AMD integrated GPU for WebGL when the tool lets it (HyperFrames local render, Remotion with `--gl=angle`, or Remotion client-side rendering in a visible browser), so simple Three.js device mockups should render acceptably. The software-GL penalty applies mainly to cloud functions and to machines whose GPU Chrome blocklists.
- Cloud functions have no GPU either, so they do not make WebGL frames cheaper to draw; they help by running many chunks in parallel and by freeing the PC.
- Practical pattern for a weak PC: preview at draft quality or reduced scale, keep blur/shadow stacks light, and send final renders to the cloud (Lambda for cents, or HyperFrames' five free hosted exports a month).

## 6. Family B — performance and motion transfer from a webcam or phone video

### Takeaway
Kling Motion Control is the best-documented and cheapest verified option: a character image plus a 3–30 second motion video, at $0.07–$0.17 per second at API rates (roughly $0.70–$1.70 per 10-second clip). Runway Act-Two is still the only model on Runway's character-performance API (3–30 s driving video, 720p-class output). Alibaba's open-source Wan2.2-Animate (Sep 2025) and Wan-Animate-2 (Aug 2026) do the same job without licence fees but are tuned for multi-GPU servers, so a weak-PC user can only reach them through hosted services.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** Kling Motion Control (documented for 2.6): transfers motion from a reference video to a character image; reference video 3–30 s; orientation mode `video` (up to 30 s, best for full-body dance) or `image` (up to 10 s, best for portraits with camera moves); standard = 720p, pro = 1080p; inputs at least 720 px and the character must show head, shoulders and torso. Weaknesses: "Flat 2D cartoons may struggle with back views"; if limbs are hidden "the AI will hallucinate hands, often leading to artifacts"; matching framing and aspect ratio between image and video helps — [ComfyUI Kling Motion Control doc](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/kling/kling-motion-control.mdx)
- **[P]** Kling 3.0 also has motion control with the same 30 s / 10 s orientation limits. ComfyUI rates: v2.6 14.77 (std) / 23.63 (pro); v3 26.59 (std) / 35.45 (pro) credits per second — [ComfyUI Kling 3.0 doc](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/kling/kling-3-0.mdx); [ComfyUI pricing](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/pricing.mdx)
- **[S]** Kling's own API prices: 2.6 Motion Control $0.07/s standard; 3.0 Motion Control $0.126/s standard, $0.168/s pro (page last verified 29 May 2026) — [Kling API pricing](https://kling.ai/document-api/pricing/base/video.md)
- **[P]** Runway Act-Two: `act_two` is the only allowed model on Runway's `character_performance` endpoint in the current SDK. Inputs: a character image or video ("A visually recognizable face must be visible and stay within the frame") and a reference performance video of 3 to 30 seconds. Options: body control (gestures as well as face), expression intensity 1–5. Output ratios are 720p-class (1280:720, 720:1280, 960:960, 1104:832, 832:1104, 1584:672) — [Runway SDK character_performance params](https://github.com/runwayml/sdk-python/blob/main/src/runwayml/types/character_performance_create_params.py)
- **[P]** Wan2.2-Animate-14B (released 19 Sep 2025, Apache 2.0): "an unified model for character animation and replacement" — animation mode makes the character image mimic the input video; replacement mode swaps the character into the video. In ComfyUI it needs pose/face preprocessing and extends in 77-frame (about 4.8 s) chunks; the guide warns to "use a small size… in case you don't have enough VRAM" — [Wan2.2 repo](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.2); [ComfyUI Wan2.2 Animate doc](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/video/wan/wan2-2-animate.mdx)
- **[P]** Wan-Animate-2 (weights released 7 Aug 2026, Apache 2.0): end-to-end, "directly consumes driving videos" with no pose extraction; adds text-driven viewpoint control and can generate a new background from the prompt; a Lite variant targets real-time streaming. Defaults are "tuned for 8× A800 GPUs" for 720P (480P tested on 2× A800). ComfyUI generates 81 frames (about 3.4 s at 24 fps) per loop — [Wan-Animate-2 repo](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan-Animate-2); [ComfyUI Wan Animate 2 doc](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/video/wan/wan-animate-2.mdx)
- **[P]** Wan-Dancer (2026, Apache 2.0): music plus a reference image to dance video, "stable, 720p/30fps videos exceeding one minute"; tested on 8× A800 80GB — [Wan-Dancer repo](https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan-Dancer)
- **[S]** The Wan-Animate paper (17 Sep 2025) claims it outperforms Animate Anyone, VACE and commercial solutions (authors' claim) — [paper summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2509.14055)
- **[P]** Higgsfield Genjutsu also covers recasting ("Recast a phone clip with a different character") but at $0.45–$2.33 per second on the derived conversion — [ComfyUI Genjutsu doc](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/higgsfield/genjutsu-motion-transfer.mdx)

### Inferences
- Cost per 10-second performance clip at API list rates: Kling 2.6 about $0.70 (720p) or $1.12 (1080p); Kling 3.0 about $1.26 or $1.68.
- A phone or webcam recording is sufficient input; the documented failure modes (hidden hands, mismatched framing, back views on flat cartoon characters) are avoidable by shooting to match the character image.
- Act-Two's 720p-class output is adequate for vertical social video but would need upscaling for sharp 1080p delivery.
- The open-source Wan animation models are out of reach locally on integrated graphics; their value to this user is that they keep hosted prices low.

### Gaps
- **Runway Act-Two's price and Viggle (and its successors) could not be verified.** Unverified background from before mid-2026: Act-Two was billed per second of output in Runway credits on paid plans, and Viggle had a free watermarked tier with paid plans from roughly $10/month. Treat both as unknown until checked.
- No independent comparison of facial fidelity, hand accuracy or long-clip stability between Kling Motion Control, Act-Two and Wan Animate was reachable.

## 7. World models and AI 3D-scene generators

### Takeaway
Only partly verifiable. World Labs' Marble is live with an API (models "Marble 1.1" and "Marble 1.1 Plus") and produces Gaussian-splat worlds that an agent can import into Blender 5.3+ and that render in Three.js through World Labs' MIT-licensed Spark library, so it can supply 3D environments to Family A workflows. I could not verify Marble's current pricing or anything about Google Genie's availability or cost, so whether either is practical for social video today remains open.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** MCP for Blender's `generate_gaussian_world` tool calls World Labs; the world "arrives as Gaussian splats, with a hidden collision mesh for placing objects and the panorama as the world background, in metres with the ground at z=0". "It needs Blender 5.3 or newer, which imports and renders splats". It runs through the add-on's paid Premium tier or your own World Labs API key ("API credits are separate from Marble app credits"), with a choice of Marble 1.1 Plus (default, larger worlds) or Marble 1.1 — [MCP for Blender](https://github.com/ahujasid/mcp-for-blender)
- **[P]** Spark, by World Labs: "An advanced 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer for THREE.js"; MIT; about 3.9k stars; version 2.4.0; reads PLY, SPZ, SPLAT, KSPLAT and SOG; "Works across almost all devices, targeting 98%+ WebGL2 support" and "Renders fast even on low-powered mobile devices" (vendor claims) — [Spark repo](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark)
- **[P]** Neither World Labs nor Google Genie appears among the 47 vendor sections of ComfyUI's partner-node price list (synced 16 Nov 2026) — [ComfyUI pricing](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/pricing.mdx)

### Inferences
- The most plausible low-cost use for this creator is as a background generator: create one splat world, load it in a Three.js scene with Spark, and fly a scripted camera through it inside a Remotion or HyperFrames composition — a path that should work on integrated graphics given Spark's mobile-class targets. This is untested.
- Splat worlds tend to look best as environments seen from a limited range of viewpoints; they do not supply animated characters or products, so they complement rather than replace Families A and B.
- Interactive world models (the Genie type) produce explorable sessions rather than directed, editable shots, which makes them a poor match for fast, repeatable social clips until export and control are verified.

### Gaps
- **Marble plan prices, generation allowances, export formats and commercial-use terms are unverified** (worldlabs.ai blocked). Unverified background from before mid-2026: Marble launched in November 2025 with a free tier and paid tiers of roughly $20–$95/month.
- **Google Genie's current version, access route, price and regional availability are unverified.** Unverified background: Genie 3 was opened in early 2026 as an experimental "Project Genie" for US subscribers of Google's top-tier AI plan, with short, time-limited sessions.

## 8. Bottom line — where each family is the fastest and cheapest route to a professional result, and where it is a poor fit

### Takeaway
For a solo creator on integrated graphics, Family A is the default for anything designed — launch videos, UI and device-mockup showcases, logo and 3D-text stings, charts and explainers — at $0–$20 a month and minutes per render. Family B is the paid add-on (about $1–$4 per 10-second attempt) for the few shots that need photoreal or character imagery with controlled motion. World models and Blender-by-agent are niche for this profile.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** Software cost for Family A is zero for an individual: Remotion free licence; HyperFrames Apache 2.0 with free local rendering — [Remotion LICENSE.md](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md); [HyperFrames FAQ](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/faq.mdx)
- **[P]** Agent cost: Claude Pro $20/month ($17/month annual) includes Claude Code; HyperFrames Studio can run free models with no sign-in — [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [HyperFrames free-agents guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/free-agents.mdx)
- **[P]** Speed (vendor claim): an agent builds a launch video in "about 10 to 15 minutes"; realistic polished work takes far more prompts per creator reports — [product-launch-video guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/product-launch-video.mdx); **[S]** [Demo Editor](https://demoeditor.com/articles/remotion-claude-code-launch-video)
- **[P]** Hardware: rendering is offline and frame-exact, usable without a GPU, with cloud renders for cents (Remotion Lambda $0.017–$0.021 for a 1-minute video) or 5 free hosted exports a month (HyperFrames) — [Remotion GPU doc](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/gpu.mdx); [Lambda cost examples](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/packages/docs/docs/lambda/cost-example.mdx); [HyperFrames FAQ](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/faq.mdx)
- **[P]** Family B is cloud-only and priced per second: 12.66 to 91.15 ComfyUI credits per second for mainstream edit models, 14.77–35.45 for Kling Motion Control (list synced 16 Nov 2026) — [ComfyUI pricing](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/tutorials/partner-nodes/pricing.mdx)
- **[P]** Quality ceiling of Family A depends on model and iteration: "The top models are still better at the hard parts: timing, composition, finish" — [HyperFrames free-agents guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/free-agents.mdx)

### Inferences
- **Family A is the fastest, cheapest professional route for:** product and app launch videos; UI walkthroughs with 3D phone/laptop mockups; logo and 3D-text stings; kinetic typography; charts and data stories; map flyovers; code animations; maths and concept explainers (Manim); captioned audiograms. Recommended starting stack for this user: HyperFrames Studio (no terminal, Windows installer, free agent option) or Claude Code + Remotion (largest ecosystem, official 3D template); add React Three Fiber/Three.js only for the shots that need real 3D.
- **Family A is a poor fit for:** character animation, faces, photoreal scenes, organic motion, and anything sold on "cinematic" imagery. Agent-driven Blender on integrated graphics is the least practical sub-option; Motion Canvas is stale.
- **Family B is the right tool for:** turning a phone or webcam performance into a stylised or 3D-looking character (Kling Motion Control, Act-Two); restyling a rough blockout, screen capture or phone clip into a photoreal or stylised render while keeping its camera and timing (Kling O1/Omni edit, Wan 2.7 edit, Gemini Omni Flash edit and Grok edit at the cheap end; Runway Aleph2, Luma Ray 3.2 edit and Kling 4.0 edit at the premium end); relighting and background replacement (Beeble SwitchX).
- **Family B is a poor fit for:** text- or UI-heavy shots, long continuous takes (most inputs cap at 15–30 seconds), and tight budgets with many retries.
- **Best combined workflow:** build the whole piece in code (titles, UI, mockups, timing, sound), and drop in a small number of Family B clips — restyled blockouts or motion-transferred characters — as footage layers. This keeps per-video spend near $0–$5 while lifting the "3D" look beyond what code alone gives.
- **Cost summary (monthly billing unless stated):** Family A $0–$20/month all-in; Family B roughly $0.60–$4.30 per 10-second attempt at API list rates (derived conversion), more through vendor apps; hosted or serverless final renders are cents.

### Gaps
- The weakest evidence in these notes: weak-PC render times (no benchmark), Family B subscription prices (third-party only), and everything about Viggle, Flow Studio, Intangible, Krea video, Act-Two pricing, Marble pricing, Genie and regional (EU/EEA, UK) restrictions (not verifiable in this session).
- To close these, the following hosts need to be reachable or searched: remotion.dev, blender.org, docs.github.com, runwayml.com (and help/docs subdomains), lumalabs.ai, kling.ai, krea.ai, viggle.ai, autodesk.com, intangible.ai, worldlabs.ai, deepmind.google / labs.google, huggingface.co, x.com.
