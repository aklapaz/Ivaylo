## 1. Blender on integrated graphics: current version, requirements, EEVEE vs Cycles on CPU, learning curve, shortcuts

### Takeaway
Blender 5.3 (released 10 November 2026) is the current release and 5.2 LTS (14 July 2026, supported to July 2028) is the long-term one; the official minimum is a 4-core SSE4.2 CPU, 8 GB RAM and a 2 GB-VRAM GPU with OpenGL 4.3/Vulkan, with Intel graphics from Kaby Lake onward and AMD GCN 4th-gen onward officially supported — so it installs and runs on most integrated graphics, but Cycles GPU rendering is not offered for Intel integrated chips and Cycles on a laptop CPU is far too slow for animation, leaving EEVEE (or simpler viewport-style rendering) as the only practical route. No 2026 measurement of EEVEE animation rendering on integrated graphics was found, so the render-time figures in "Inferences" are arithmetic scenarios, not benchmarks; learning-time sources are weak but agree on roughly tens of hours to basic competence and many months to a year or more for professional-level work.

The page says to always install the latest drivers from the manufacturer, and that older GPUs may still run Blender but are not supported. [VS] — [blender.org Requirements](https://www.blender.org/download/requirements/)
- Blender 5.0 raised these minimums because the older hardware is no longer supported by its manufacturers; Blender 4.5 LTS still supports NVIDIA GeForce 400 and newer and is the last release to support Intel Macs (macOS 11.2). #### How integrated graphics behave in practice
- Bug report on EEVEE Next: the default scene ran at around 1 fps on an Intel UHD 620; a Blender developer replied that EEVEE Next is "not optimized for these kinds of devices" and that the team was looking into making it workable on low-end hardware. [OLD: predates mid-2026] — [Blender issue #114597](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/114597)
- User report: Blender 4.5's Vulkan backend did not work on a Windows 10 ThinkPad with UHD 620 but worked on the same laptop under Fedora Linux; the post also notes Intel has stopped feature updates for UHD 620 and other 6th–10th-generation integrated GPUs. [3P user report, OLD: 2025] — [Lenovo community post](https://gaming.lenovo.com/general/post/new-blender-4-5-with-vulkan-support-works-on-older-6th-10th-gen-intel-nvkbgySOpuftTMb)
- Pre-release coverage of the 5.3 Vulkan transition says Intel 6th–10th-generation integrated GPUs are listed as unsupported on Windows (Linux with Mesa is listed as a supported path) and describes a plan to drop OpenGL ("Vulkan only") on a timeline running from 5.3 to 6.0; Blender still falls back to OpenGL when Vulkan is unavailable. (Attribution taken from the search summary; the articles were not opened and describe the in-development stage.) [3P] — [XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/blender-opengl-removal-vulkan-textures/); [HWBusters](https://hwbusters.com/?p=118866)
- Older threads report EEVEE drawing incorrectly or crashing on Intel HD 620 / UHD 630-class laptops and severe viewport lag on Ice Lake / Iris Plus laptops; some users said Linux fixed it. [OLD: 2018–2020] — [Blender Artists: HD 620 and EEVEE](https://blenderartists.org/t/intel-hd-graphics-620-dont-work-with-eevee/1119230); [Blender bug T70922](https://developer.blender.org/T70922); [Blender Artists: Surface Pro 7](https://blenderartists.org/t/blender-not-working-well-on-surface-pro-7/1187133)
- Official troubleshooting advice for graphics glitches and crashes is to update the graphics drivers. [VS] — [Blender Manual, Graphics Hardware troubleshooting](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/troubleshooting/gpu/)
- Integrated GPUs depend heavily on dual-channel memory: Notebookcheck reports graphics performance dropping by as much as 40–50% with single-channel RAM on Radeon 780M / Arc 140V-class chips (gaming and 3DMark tests, not Blender). [3P] — [Notebookcheck](https://notebookcheck.net/dynabook-tecra-a65-m-shows-the-importance-of-dual-channel-ram-for-integrated-graphics-performance.1208012.0.html)
- Newer integrated GPUs are much faster than UHD / Iris Xe in general graphics tests: Notebookcheck called the Arc 140V (Lunar Lake) the fastest iGPU it had tested in Time Spy and faster and more efficient than the Radeon 890M (not a Blender test). [3P, OLD: 2024] — [Notebookcheck Lunar Lake iGPU analysis](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Intel-Lunar-Lake-iGPU-analysis-Arc-Graphics-140V-is-faster-and-more-efficient-than-Radeon-890M.894167.0.html)
- The only EEVEE benchmark series surfaced (Techgage, Blender 3.x) used discrete cards and found Intel trailing AMD and NVIDIA in EEVEE and viewport tests. [3P, OLD: 2022–2023] — [Techgage, Blender 3.4 deep-dive](https://techgage.com/article/blender-3-4-performance-deep-dive-cycles-eevee-viewport/2)
- A proposed EEVEE optimisation reported in March 2026 could roughly double performance in CPU-bottlenecked, instancing-heavy scenes; it was a pending pull request at the time and I could not confirm it shipped. [NEWS Mar 2026] — [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Blender-EEVEE-2x-Perf?=0)

### Inferences
- **Which integrated chips are inside the official support range (inference from the requirements page plus general hardware knowledge, not a test):** Intel 7th-generation (Kaby Lake) and later — UHD 620/630, Iris Plus, Iris Xe, integrated Arc — and AMD Ryzen integrated graphics (Vega and later are newer than GCN 4th gen) nominally qualify; Intel 6th-generation (Skylake) and older AMD A-series graphics do not. "Qualifies" only means Blender starts and is supported; it says nothing about speed.
- **Old Intel chips on Windows are the risk case.** The evidence on UHD 620-class graphics (1 fps in EEVEE Next; Vulkan failing on Windows 10; 6th–10th-gen iGPUs reportedly unsupported for Vulkan on Windows; OpenGL slated for removal by 6.0) suggests a 7th–10th-gen Intel laptop is a poor Blender machine now and has a limited future. For such a machine, staying on 5.2 LTS (supported to July 2028) rather than tracking 5.3+ is the conservative choice.
- **8 GB RAM is exactly the official minimum, and an integrated GPU takes its video memory out of that same RAM**, so 8 GB machines will be tight; 16 GB in dual-channel is the realistic floor for comfortable work (inference from the 8 GB minimum / 32 GB recommendation and the Notebookcheck dual-channel finding).
- **Cycles is effectively CPU-only on Intel integrated graphics** (oneAPI is documented for Arc cards with Xe HPG only), and CPU Cycles is impractical for animation: at the one measured figure, 946 s per frame, a 10–15 s clip (240–450 frames at 24–30 fps) would take about 63–118 hours. That figure is for a heavy benchmark scene, so it is an upper-end illustration, but even a scene ten times lighter would need roughly 6–12 hours.
- **Render-time scenarios for a 10–15 s 1080p clip (pure arithmetic; which row applies to a given iGPU is NOT established by any source found):**

  | Seconds per frame | 10 s at 24 fps (240 frames) | 15 s at 30 fps (450 frames) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 4 min | 7.5 min |
  | 5 | 20 min | 37.5 min |
  | 15 | 1 h | 1 h 53 min |
  | 30 | 2 h | 3 h 45 min |
  | 60 | 4 h | 7 h 30 min |
  | 300 (5 min) | 20 h | 37.5 h |
  | 946 (Classroom on i7-1355U, Cycles CPU) | about 63 h | about 118 h |

- **Learning time for this user (judgement from weak sources):** a first presentable clip built from ready-made assets or a template plausibly takes on the order of 10–20 focused hours; consistent, "professional-looking" original work takes months and commonly around a year of regular practice. Asset libraries, AI-generated models and the MCP-style assistants shorten modeling and scene assembly, but none of the sources shows them removing the need to learn lighting, camera animation and render settings.
- **Blender itself costs nothing; the optional paid shortcut is BlenderKit Full at about USD 9–10 per month.**

### Gaps
- **No 2026 measurement of EEVEE (or viewport-render) animation speed on Intel UHD / Iris Xe / integrated Arc or AMD Radeon integrated graphics was found.** This is the single most important missing number for this question. Blender Open Data (opendata.blender.org) could not be queried.
- Whether AMD HIP (Cycles GPU) works on Radeon integrated graphics, and whether Intel oneAPI works on integrated Arc (Meteor Lake / Lunar Lake / Panther Lake) in 5.2–5.3, could not be confirmed; the manual wording ("graphics card", "Xe HPG") suggests not officially.
- Vulkan minimum version (1.1 vs 1.3) and the final 5.3 status of Intel 6th–10th-gen iGPUs on Windows are unresolved; the OpenGL-removal timeline comes from article titles and a search summary only.
- The Windows minimum ("Windows 8.1") comes from a search snippet of the requirements page that the tool marked as possibly stale; verify on the live page.
- Poly Haven's licence (widely known to be CC0) and asset counts were not confirmed in this session. Sketchfab's 2026 status is unknown. "Blendkit" may be a rename of BlenderKit — not verified.
- No reliable source on hours-to-competence was found; a figure of "1,500–2,000 hours according to a 2023 Autodesk study" surfaced on a low-quality page and could not be verified — do not use it.
- Not researched (budget): ready-made Blender product-animation templates and paid add-ons; newer AI assistants for Blender beyond Blender MCP; whether the March 2026 EEVEE optimisation shipped.
- **UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND (researcher's memory, roughly 2024–2025; not checked in this session):** Blender also has the Workbench render engine (the solid-viewport renderer), which renders far faster than EEVEE and gives a flat, clay or toon-like look that can suit stylized social clips on weak hardware; typical EEVEE weak-hardware tactics are lowering samples and disabling ray tracing and volumetrics. My expectation — not a measurement — is that simple stylized EEVEE scenes on Iris Xe / Radeon-class integrated graphics land in the "seconds to a few tens of seconds per 1080p frame" rows of the table above, and simple Cycles-CPU scenes in the "one to several minutes per frame" rows.

## 2. Browser-based 3D tools with animation and video export (Spline, Womp, Vectary, Bezi and others)

### Takeaway
Spline is the only browser tool in this set with documented timeline animation plus MP4 video export, but video export starts at the Pro plan (USD 25 per seat per month billed annually, USD 30 monthly); Womp's pricing page lists Full-HD image and video export on its free Starter plan and 4K/60 fps video on Pro (USD 9.99 per month billed annually, with conflicting monthly prices), though keyframe animation is still described as an experimental Pro feature; Vectary shows no evidence of video export, and Bezi has left this category to become an AI assistant for Unity developers. No source on how any of these performs on integrated graphics was found.

## 7. Bottom line for the true-3D route: cost, time to learn, time per clip, quality ceiling versus AI video generators, when it is worth it

### Takeaway
On the prices found, true 3D can be done for USD 0 and realistically for about USD 10–50 per month once one or two paid tools are added, so money is not the obstacle; the obstacles for a creator with integrated graphics are learning time (tens of hours to a first decent Blender result, months to a year for consistently professional work) and render time (unmeasured for EEVEE on integrated graphics; prohibitive for Cycles on a laptop CPU). True 3D is justified when the content needs exact product geometry, reusable brand assets, precise control and perfect consistency; it is the wrong first choice when the priority is speed and minimal effort. The comparison with AI video generators here is inference: no head-to-head source was retrieved, and that side is covered by other researchers.

### Inferences
- **Total cost, three realistic stacks (arithmetic on the cited prices):**
  - Zero-cost: Blender + free half of BlenderKit / Poly Haven + Meshy free tier (attribution, tight download limits) + Womp Starter for quick browser renders = USD 0 per month.
  - Lean paid: Blender + Meshy Pro in the months assets are needed = USD 20 per month; adding BlenderKit Full = about USD 30 per month.
  - Browser-first: Spline Pro on monthly billing = USD 30 per month (USD 300 per year on annual billing); with Meshy Pro = USD 50 per month.
- **Time to learn (judgement, low confidence):** template tools — under an hour; Spline or Womp — a few hours to a first clip, days to weeks to fluency; Blender — roughly 10–20 hours to a first decent clip using ready-made assets, months to about a year for reliably professional results.
- **Time per finished 15–30 s clip (judgement, low confidence; not measured):** template mockup — minutes to about an hour; browser 3D tool with a reusable scene — about one to a few hours; Blender — several hours of work for a new scene, falling to an hour or two when a brand scene is reused, plus render time. A 15–30 s clip is 360–900 frames at 24–30 fps, so render time on integrated graphics runs from roughly a quarter of an hour to an hour and a quarter (if a frame takes 2–5 s) to 3–15 hours (if a frame takes 30–60 s), and to days with Cycles on CPU. Until someone measures EEVEE on the actual machine, the render-time risk is unquantified.
- **Quality ceiling versus AI video generators (judgement):** true 3D wins on determinism — the same model, colours, logo and camera move every time, any duration, no morphing or drifting details — but on integrated graphics the achievable look is real-time-style (EEVEE, browser WebGL), not path-traced photorealism, and it costs far more skilled effort per second of video. Whether current AI video generators beat that look per hour and per dollar is for the other researchers' notes; nothing retrieved here measures it.
- **When true 3D is worth it:** an exact product shape must be shown correctly (packaging, devices, hardware); the same brand asset or mascot will appear across many posts; precise timing, camera paths, text or logo placement matter; clips must match each other perfectly; or interactive/web reuse of the same 3D asset is planned. In these cases the up-front learning and scene-building cost is amortised over many clips.
- **When it is the wrong choice:** one-off or trend-driven posts; cinematic, organic or photoreal scenes (people, nature, complex environments); a creator who needs output this week with no prior 3D experience; any plan that depends on Cycles-quality rendering without a dedicated GPU or rented compute; any plan built on Unreal Engine with integrated graphics.
- **Most defensible path for this specific user, on the evidence available:** start with a template or browser tool for device/packaging-style clips (Womp Starter, Pixcap, Pacdora, Rotato web beta; Spline Pro for a month if real keyframe animation is needed), use an AI 3D generator for custom assets, and treat Blender (5.2 LTS, EEVEE) as a medium-term investment to be tested first on the actual laptop with a short render before committing.
