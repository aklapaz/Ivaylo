## 1. X's paid tiers and Grok Imagine: prices, what each tier includes, limits, free access, quality and regional limits

### Takeaway
An X subscription covers AI video only from the **$30 Premium tier** upward, according to several third-party readings of xAI's plan table; xAI's own documentation (read directly) confirms the 12 October 2026 restructuring into Free, Plus $8, Premium $30, Super $100 and Ultra $200 per month, but does not publish what each tier gets in Grok Imagine. No tier has a clip count (one weekly usage pool at 1x, 4x, 15x and 50x), every clip made in the Grok apps carries a Grok watermark that cannot be switched off, 1080p is advertised from the $100 tier, and independent blind votes put Grok Imagine Video 1.5 mid-table (17th of 35 for image-to-video and 18th in the animation category on Artificial Analysis).

### Inferences
- **For an X-first creator the $8 Plus plan does not replace a video tool.** What it buys is on the publishing side (checkmark, "creator tools", fewer ads, and 1080p upload and playback per section 2) plus, according to third parties, Grok Imagine stills. Video through X starts at $30 per month ($25 on annual billing), six times the price of Google AI Plus ($4.99, which includes video generation: see section 6 sources).
- **The watermark is the strongest argument against relying on the Grok apps for "professional-looking" output.** xAI's FAQ says every app-generated clip is watermarked and removal breaches its terms. Whether API output (xAI, fal.ai, kie.ai) carries a visible mark is not documented; a one-clip test would settle it.
- **Pay-per-use is cheaper than the $30 tier at low volume.** On the arithmetic used in the first round (a 20-second piece of four shots, 60–80 generated seconds), the Lite model at 720p costs $1.80–$2.40 per piece and the full 1.5 model $8.40–$11.20. Twelve Lite pieces cost about the same as one month of Premium, without the unknown quota.
- kie.ai's rate works out to about $0.012, $0.0225 and $0.04 per second at the $0.005-per-credit value recorded in the aggregators note. That is below xAI's own Lite prices at 1080p, so I would treat the model variant behind it as unconfirmed.
- **Quality is adequate, not leading.** On AA the 1.5 model sits roughly 115 Elo points behind the image-to-video leader (about a 66/34 preference split) and about level with Gemini Omni Flash 1.1; cheaper MiniMax-H3-based models rank above it (section 4). Its real advantages are practical: 15-second clips, 9:16, native lip-synced audio and up to 14 reference images.
- The conflict about free access is best read as a sequence: no free Imagine from January–March 2026, then a small free image allowance under the October plan. This is my reading of sources of different dates, not a confirmed fact.

## 2. X as a publishing platform: upload limits, AI-media labelling and creator payouts

### Takeaway
X's developer documentation (read directly) now states that an account without Premium can post video up to 20 minutes and 8 GB, and a Premium or verified account up to 125 minutes and 16 GB, with 1080p upload and playback reserved for subscribers and 720p for everyone else; this is looser than the 140-second free limit still quoted from X's help centre, and a 15–30-second clip fits under either. AI labelling remains a voluntary "Made with AI" disclosure (the posting API has a matching `made_with_ai` field) with one mandatory case, and the payout programme still does not say whether AI-generated video counts as "original".

### Cited Findings
- [READ, OFFICIAL] Size and duration limits "enforced first by the upload endpoints, then again when you attach a media_id to a Post". Default accounts (no X Premium): video on a post 0.5 seconds–**20 minutes**, **8 GB**. X Premium or verified accounts: up to **125 minutes**, **16 GB**. Direct-message video: 140 seconds and 512 MB by default, 10 minutes and 1 GB with Premium. "Post-video caps match the X app." Limits follow the posting user's status, not the developer plan. — [X developer docs: media best practices](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)
- [READ, OFFICIAL] The changelog entry documenting these limits is dated **1 September 2026**. — [X developer docs: changelog](https://docs.x.com/changelog)
- [READ, OFFICIAL] "Subscribed users can upload a 1080p video and get 1080p playback. Unsubscribed users can upload a 720p video and get a 720p playback." Recommended: H.264 High Profile, 30 or 60 fps, 1280×720, 720×1280 or 720×720, at least 5,000 kbps video and 128 kbps AAC-LC audio, aspect ratio 16:9 (landscape or portrait) or 1:1. Hard limits: 60 fps or less, aspect ratio between 1:3 and 3:1, YUV 4:2:0, progressive scan, no open GOP. The same page still lists "dimensions must be between 32x32 and 1280x1024", which sits oddly with 1080p for subscribers. — [X developer docs: media best practices](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)
- [SS, third-party guides, 2026, conflicting] Guides agree on 140 seconds and 512 MB for free accounts and disagree on paid limits (2, 3 or 4 hours on web and iOS; 8 or 16 GB; 10 minutes on Android). — [SuperX](https://superx.so/blog/twitter-video-length-limit); [Mallary](https://www.mallary.ai/blog/how-long-can-a-video-be-on-twitter); [OpenTweet](https://opentweet.io/answers/x-api-video-upload-limit) (≈)
- [READ, OFFICIAL] The Create Post endpoint has an optional boolean `made_with_ai` ("Disclose that the tweet contains AI-generated media") next to `paid_partnership` ("Disclose that the tweet is a paid partnership"; added 3 June 2026). — [X developer docs: create post](https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/create-post); [X developer docs: changelog](https://docs.x.com/changelog)
- [SS, PRESS, OLD (March 2026)] No general mandatory AI label was found. The one mandatory case, announced on 3–4 March 2026, covers creators in X's payout programme who post AI-generated videos of armed conflict without disclosure: 90-day suspension from payouts, permanent removal on repetition. Enforcement relies on detection tools, metadata signals and Community Notes. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/x-to-require-ai-labels-on-armed-conflict-videos-from-paid-creators-citing-times-of-war-183631400.html); [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/x-will-stop-paying-people-for-sharing-unlabeled-ai-generated-war-footage/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/x-suspends-creators-unlabeled-ai-war-videos/)
- [SS, OFFICIAL] The Original Content Rewards help page lists content "created or posted using automated means" among content that is not original, defines original content as what "you personally create", and requires an active X Premium, Premium+ or Premium Business subscription; the new tier names do not appear on it. Qualified impressions are unique impressions from subscribers. — [X Help: Original Content Rewards](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)

### Inferences
- X's two official sources disagree on the free-account video limit (help centre: 140 seconds and 512 MB, per the post-production note; developer documentation dated 1 September 2026: 20 minutes and 8 GB, "match the X app"). The developer page is the newer and more explicit one, and the 140-second figure now appears there only for direct messages, so the help-centre figure may be stale. This does not affect a 15–30-second clip.
- **On a free X account 720p is all that plays back**, so the 720p output of the cheap video models loses nothing on X without a subscription. 1080p only pays off with a paid X plan and on the other platforms.
- The $8 Plus plan probably satisfies the payout programme's subscription condition, because xAI's documentation says paid individual plans "include X Premium benefits" and gives Plus "creator tools". X's own payout page has not been updated to say so; treat as likely, not confirmed.
- The `made_with_ai` API field shows the label is a first-class, creator-set property of a post. Using it costs nothing and is the safe choice for realistic content.

### Gaps
- help.x.com could not be opened, so the payout rules and the consumer upload table were not re-read. The payout thresholds recorded in the post-production note (500 verified followers and 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in 90 days) were not re-verified here.
- Which of the new plans (Plus, Premium, Super, Ultra) qualifies for Original Content Rewards, and whether AI-generated video counts as original: not stated anywhere I could find.

### Inferences
- **Planning hit rate.** Use 3–4 generations per usable shot when the keyframe is locked and the motion is simple (the vendor logs), and 5–15 for a beginner or for complex action (the relayed creator reports). The first round's "3–4" is therefore an optimistic floor for the first weeks.
- **Time for a 15–30-second piece (my estimate, low confidence).** Machine waiting alone: four shots at 3–4 attempts each is 12–16 generations, or about 18–32 minutes one after another on a model that takes 1.5–2 minutes per clip, and about an hour on Seedance; a beginner at 10 attempts per shot waits 1–2.5 hours. Scaling the vendor figures for 3-minute shorts down to 20 seconds gives roughly 1.5–3.5 hours of total work; fixed tasks (planning, character sheet, sound, captions) do not shrink in proportion, so 2–4 hours is a fair first-month expectation and under an hour is realistic only for a single-shot piece. This matches the first round's own estimate; neither rests on a creator's log.
- **Budget (my arithmetic).** Video for one 20-second piece (60–80 generated seconds) costs about $1.80–$2.40 at $0.03 per second (HeyGen Video 1 at 768p, Grok Imagine 1.5 Lite at 720p), $2.10–$2.80 on Pika H3 Flex, $3–$4 on Veo 3.1 Lite and $4.80–$6.40 on MiniMax H3. At a beginner's 10 attempts per shot (200 seconds) the same piece costs $6–$16. Eight pieces a month therefore cost roughly $15–$50 on pay-per-use, or a $5–$20 subscription plus occasional top-ups.
- **The one subscription.** No independent consensus exists. On the evidence in these notes the lowest-risk single purchase is Google AI Plus at $4.99 (video in Flow and the Gemini app, plus the Nano Banana models), accepting that its video models rank low for image-to-video animation (section 4); the best quality per dollar is pay-per-use access to a MiniMax-H3-family model. This is my inference from prices and rankings, not a creator's recommendation.
- **Subscriptions are quota bets.** Credit prices move without notice (the Dreamina and Kling examples above, and the Adobe thread in section 3), which supports monthly rather than annual billing.

- Independent monthly-spend figures: none beyond the vendor surveys already in the first-round note.
- Hit rates by model and by archetype (diorama, product, character dialogue): not found.
- The second-hand Reddit claims could not be checked against the original threads.

- **A beginner does not need to pay for stills.** The Gemini app and Flow together cover the first-round keyframe workflow (character sheet, storyboard grid, start and end frames) with Nano Banana models, and ChatGPT adds the top-ranked model family for hero frames. The scores in the table are for API settings; free consumer tiers may run lower quality settings.
- **Check each output for a visible mark before animating it.** A visible watermark in the start frame will be carried into the video. Grok is the clearest case and its terms forbid removal; for Gemini and Dreamina the user should check their own downloads.
- For image-to-video, generate the start frame at the aspect ratio and resolution of the target video (AA's own test images are 1920×1080 PNGs matched to the output), since the video model inherits the frame.
- If free limits bind, paid stills remain a rounding error: Nano Banana 2.1 costs about $0.034 per image and Seedream 5.5 about $0.02 on the AA price list.
