# Animate Stills Cheaply, Assemble Them in Remotion

As of November 2026 the cheapest professional-looking route for a solo creator on an integrated-graphics PC needs no new all-in-one AI subscription. The creator should keep the tools already paid for as the spine and rent only the motion. That means making the still image for free (Nano Banana in Google Flow, or a Blender render of the creator's own model), animating it pay-as-you-go on fal.ai with **MiniMax H3 at 768p ($0.06 per second)** or **HeyGen Video 1 ($0.02 per second)**, keeping **Seedance 2.5** (the blind-vote leader, about 8 to 24 times the price) for one hero shot, voicing and scoring it in ElevenLabs, and assembling, captioning and branding it in Remotion with a coding agent. A finished 20-second vertical clip then costs roughly **$1.50 to $4 in generation** and takes 2 to 4 hours in the first weeks. Once a Remotion template exists it drops to about an hour, because every generator runs in the cloud and the weak PC only renders the final edit. The X posts that promise "3D in minutes" time a single clip, not a finished video: real hit rates are 3 to 15 generations per usable shot, and much of the showcase material is sponsored. For the engineering channel the rule is the reverse. AI may supply mood and atmosphere, but it must never show how a machine works. The CadQuery-to-Blender pipeline stays the source of truth, and the speed gains come from reusing renders and moving simple mechanical shots into Three.js inside Remotion, not from generative video. The things most often missed are that stylised clips need no AI label while realistic AI footage of plausible machines does under EU law for a monetising creator, that raw AI clips are not protected by copyright in the US, that free tiers are almost always non-commercial or watermarked, and that X payouts require a paid subscription plus thresholds a new account will not meet for months.

## Viral "made in minutes" posts time one clip, not a finished video

Almost every "professional 3D video in minutes" workflow circulating on X follows the same pipeline. A language model writes the shot list, an image model (usually Google's Nano Banana family) makes keyframes or a storyboard grid, an image-to-video model animates 5 to 10-second clips, and an editor cuts them together ([Segmind template](https://www.segmind.com/pixelflows/dynamic-digital-illustration-animation-nano-banana-seedance-pro); [AI Fire newsletter](https://www.aifire.co/p/nano-banana-2-kling-3-0-cinematic-ai-ad-workflow-2026)). Where the "minutes" claim could be traced, it referred to generating one clip, for example a product shot "created in under 3 minutes using Kling V3.0 Pro" ([FOOH](https://www.fooh.com/blog/skims-toothpaste-ai-ad)). The only production log found shows the real ratio: one 3-minute animated episode needed **164 generated clips to get 41 usable ones**, about 25% kept ([invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/what-percentage-of-ai-generated-video-clips-are-actually/)). Creator reports relayed by guide sites run worse, at **5 to 15 generations per usable clip** for beginners ([Scenemakers Club](https://scenemakers.club/guides/ai-video-tool-prices)). Machine time adds up too. Higgsfield's measured medians are about 1.5 minutes per 8-second Kling or Wan clip and 3.5 to 4 minutes per Seedance clip ([Higgsfield blog](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/how-long-ai-video-generation-takes)).

Much of the showcase content is advertising. The Verge reported in August 2026 that prominent YouTube filmmakers posted Higgsfield/Seedance 2.5 demos not marked as ads, one with an affiliate discount link ([APH Networks mirror](https://aphnetworks.com/news/32074-major-youtube-creators-are-facing-backlash-accepting-ai-money)). The "comment a keyword and I'll DM the prompt" format is a recognised engagement-farming tactic in which the promised material may never arrive ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/if-this-22-year-old-made-usd400-000-over-the-weekend-why-cant-i-inside-the-fake-ai-entrepreneur-boom)). The code-driven trend that the creator's Remotion account belongs to has the same gap between claim and practice. A vendor guide says an agent builds a launch video in "about 10 to 15 minutes" ([HyperFrames guide](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/blob/main/docs/guides/product-launch-video.mdx)), while one founder's polished 50-second launch video took "~100 prompts, not 1" and roughly a week ([Demo Editor](https://demoeditor.com/articles/remotion-claude-code-launch-video)).

Two conclusions follow. First, the weak PC is not the bottleneck for AI video, because every generator worth using runs in the cloud. Running open-weights video models locally is not an option either: ComfyUI calls CPU mode an "absolute last resort" ([Comfy docs](https://github.com/Comfy-Org/docs/blob/main/troubleshooting/overview.mdx)), and Intel's own tool says video is "best on discrete GPUs" ([Intel AI Playground guide](https://github.com/intel/AI-Playground/blob/main/AI%20Playground%20Users%20Guide.pdf)). Second, the realistic budget for a polished 15 to 30-second multi-shot piece is **2 to 4 hours in the first month**. This is an estimate built from vendor time figures and relayed creator reports, since no independent creator log was found. A single-shot piece (one diorama or product hero shot with one camera move) can honestly be done in under an hour, because slow camera moves over a strong still fail least often.

## The best stack: Remotion as the spine, rented motion as the footage

The single best method for this creator is a **Remotion-centred hybrid**. Remotion holds everything that must be exact and reusable: titles, kinetic type, 3D text, labels, captions, logo, pacing and the music bed. AI image-to-video supplies short footage layers. ElevenLabs supplies voice, effects and music. It wins on three counts. It reuses all three paid tools instead of replacing them. It turns each new video into a variation of a template, which is the only thing that really shortens production time. And it keeps the human contribution visible, which matters for copyright, monetisation and audience trust (see the next sections).

### How one general eye-catching clip gets made

The concept and shot list come first, written with the same coding agent that drives Remotion. Next come the stills. The default is Nano Banana 2.1 in Google Flow at no cost. Nano Banana 2.1 ranks seventh on Artificial Analysis's text-to-image board and among the best for identity-preserving edits, at a quarter of Nano Banana Pro's price ([AA text-to-image](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/text-to-image); [AA image editing](https://artificialanalysis.ai/image/leaderboard/editing)). For one hero frame, OpenAI's GPT Image 2.6, the outright leader, is worth a few cents. Each still should be generated at 9:16 and the target resolution, because the video model inherits scale, focus and light from it ([Novoads](https://novoads.ai/blog/how-to-create-miniature-world-videos-with-ai)). It should also contain something that can plausibly move. Then the motion: each still goes to fal.ai's browser Playground, which runs every model from a form after prepaid credit is added ([fal docs](https://fal.ai/docs/llms-full.txt)). The default model is H3 at 768p. HeyGen Video 1 is for cheap drafts. Seedance 2.5 is for the one hero shot, drafted at 480p before a 720p final. Files must be downloaded immediately, because fal keeps outputs only "at least 7 days by default" and media URLs are public ([fal FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/faq.md)). Narration, sound effects and a single continuous music bed come from ElevenLabs, whose paid plans include the commercial licence and music commercial use ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). Finally the clips are dropped into a Remotion composition built once as a template, with the official TikTok-style word-by-word caption template and the React Three Fiber 3D template for 3D text or device shots ([Remotion templates](https://www.remotion.dev/templates)). The output is a 1080×1920 master.

Remotion's agent tooling is mature enough to make this fast. Agent Skills are now "the default way people make videos with Remotion" ([Remotion blog](https://www.remotion.dev/blog/make-videos-programmatically-interactively-and-agentically)), and the hosted Remotion MCP server was shut down on 26 October 2026 in favour of the `/remotion-docs` skill ([Remotion MCP page](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/ai/mcp)), so any older MCP-based set-up should be migrated. On Artificial Analysis's new Code2Video benchmark, which tests language models building short videos in code, Claude Opus 5.5 ranks first ([AA Code2Video-AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/code2video-aa)). That supports Claude Code as the agent. If the creator does not already have one, Claude Pro, which includes Claude Code, costs $20 a month ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). Rendering works on integrated graphics. Ordinary motion graphics are processor-bound ("Most renders do not become faster with GPU acceleration"), and Three.js scenes need the `angle` setting, which new projects preset, to reach the integrated GPU ([Remotion SSR comparison](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/compare-ssr); [Remotion @remotion/three](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three)). The one-line check is `npx remotion gpu --gl=angle` ([Remotion gpu command](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/cli/gpu)). If headless rendering of 3D is slow, the Studio's "Render in browser" button usually has the GPU available ([Remotion client-side rendering](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/client-side-rendering/)).

### What it costs and how long it takes

Video generation for the reference 20-second clip costs **$3.60 on H3, $1.20 on HeyGen Video 1, or about $7.30 with one Seedance 2.5 hero shot**. Stills cost $0 in Flow, or about $0.04 each on fal ([fal Nano Banana 2.1](https://fal.ai/models/google/nano-banana-2.1)). Audio costs well under $0.50 at ElevenLabs Starter rates, which work out to about $0.18 for a minute of narration and $0.04 per sound effect, and is effectively free inside an existing plan ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). Rendering and editing are free on the creator's own machine. The new fixed cost is **nothing**. Optional extras are a $20 to $50 prepaid fal balance a month, which covers 8 to 12 clips with retries, and **X Plus at $8 a month** for 1080p playback ([xAI docs: X Subscription pricing](https://docs.x.ai/x-subscription/pricing); [X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)). Time is about 2 to 4 hours per clip in the first weeks, and about an hour once the template, caption style and prompt patterns are fixed. Of that, 20 to 30 minutes is machine waiting, which can overlap with editing. These time figures are estimates, not measurements.

The free fallback is Flow plus Remotion with no fal spend. It works, at about three days of daily credits per finished clip, but it pays in quality: Veo 3.1 Lite and Omni Flash 1.2 rank low for animating stills. Everything else in the table loses to the hybrid on at least one decisive count. Seedance everywhere costs eight times more for a narrow edge. Higgsfield and annual plans add lock-in and weak refund terms. The Grok app adds an irremovable watermark. True 3D in Blender is too slow for generic social clips on this PC.

## Engineering explainers: AI may decorate, but must never demonstrate

The condition-monitoring channel is a different problem. Its value is correctness, and its 49-shot wind-turbine film already checks that labels land on the right part, gears turn the right way and speeds are real. Generative video cannot meet that standard, because it invents motion from pixels. Even in vendor tests, Kling 4.0 Flash's product geometry changed across cuts ([Atlas Cloud](https://www.atlascloud.ai/blog/tips/kling-4-flash-vs-seedance-2.5)). In a small product-accuracy test, Kling 3.0 kept a product correct in only 1 of 5 tries and Seedance 2.5 in 3 of 5 ([Aliteq](https://aliteq.com/ai-product-video-cost-per-usable-clip)). For a gearbox, a 60% success rate on looking right says nothing about rotation direction, gear ratio or blade-pitch behaviour, and a wrong one would damage the channel's credibility. The rule is that **no AI-generated pixel may carry a label, a direction of motion, a speed, a ratio or a part identity**. Video-to-video restyling of a Blender grey-box render is excluded on the same grounds, because restyle models re-render geometry and tend to damage small text and fine detail.

AI earns its place around the edges. It can make atmospheric establishing shots (a wind farm at dawn, a storm front, an offshore swell) and a cold-open hook before the first accurate shot. It can make abstract transitions, thumbnails and channel art, and it can draft scripts. ElevenLabs can clone the creator's own voice (instant voice cloning is included from Starter) to produce narration pick-ups without re-recording ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). A recorded voice take remains the stronger choice for authorship and audience trust. Realistic AI shots of turbines raise a legal point that stylised clips do not. Under the EU AI Act a deepfake includes AI video of objects or places that "can plausibly exist", so a photoreal AI wind farm posted by a monetising creator needs a visible AI label from the first frame ([Commission Q&A on Article 50](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). YouTube also requires disclosure of realistic synthetic scenes ([YouTube Help: disclosing GenAI](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). The creator's Blender renders are not AI output and need no label. They are also fully human-authored and protectable, which raw AI clips are not.

The speed problem therefore needs non-generative fixes. The first, and the one most easily missed, is reuse. The 49 finished shots of the turbine film can be recut into a series of vertical 15 to 45-second clips in Remotion at zero new render cost. Labels should be re-laid in code for the 9:16 frame rather than cropped, since a centre crop of a 16:9 shot will cut off annotations (an inference; nothing in the notes measures it). The second is to render stills, which already take 1 to 5 minutes each on this PC, and animate them in Remotion with camera moves, parallax and label animations, instead of rendering every moving shot overnight. The third, for simple mechanical motion, is to export the CadQuery geometry to a web 3D format and animate it in React Three Fiber inside Remotion. Remotion requires 3D animation to be driven by the frame number ([Remotion @remotion/three](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/three)), so rotation angle becomes an exact function of time and the speeds stay correct by construction. Light real-time 3D should render in minutes through `angle` on integrated graphics. That is an inference: no benchmark of Three.js on integrated GPUs exists ([Remotion --gl options](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/gl-options)). The look is real-time rather than path-traced, which suits a vertical feed.

Where the Blender look is needed, the levers are EEVEE with fewer samples and the "simplify" settings rather than Cycles ([threedee.design](https://threedee.design/blog/optimize-blender-eevee-renders-quick-tips-to-accelerate-your-3d-workflow)). The creator should run a 10-frame test first, because no measurement of EEVEE frame times on integrated graphics exists anywhere. At 5 seconds a frame a 30-second clip takes about 75 minutes; at 30 seconds a frame it takes 7.5 hours. The other option is a commercial render farm, whose roughly $25 trial credit covers a first short job. SheepIt is free but unsuitable for proprietary engineering models, because projects are visible to the volunteers who render them ([SheepIt FAQ](https://www.sheepit-renderfarm.com/faq)). The official Blender connector in Claude's directory lets an agent drive Blender's Python API ([Claude connector: Blender](https://claude.com/connectors/blender)), so scene set-up can be scripted the way the CadQuery models already are.

## What the original plan missed

### Rights, terms and watermarks decide whether a clip can be posted at all

Free tiers are almost universally unusable for monetised posting. Kling's free plan states that "generated content is not for commercial use", and its free exports are watermarked ([Kling credit guide](https://kling.ai/blog/kling-video-3-0-credit-cost-guide)). Vidu allows commercial use only for subscribers ([Vidu help centre](https://www.vidu.com/help-center)). ElevenLabs' terms say a free user "may only use the Services for non-commercial purposes" ([ElevenLabs Terms](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). The rights usually attach to output made while subscribed, so assets made on a free tier should be regenerated after paying. Paid plans fix the visible watermark on Kling, Hailuo, Vidu and Dreamina ([Hailuo subscription terms](https://hailuoai.video/doc/payment-policy.html)). Grok is the exception: every image and video from the Grok apps carries a watermark there is "no setting to remove", and removing it breaches xAI's policy ([xAI Grok FAQ](https://docs.x.ai/grok/faq)). In Flow, by contrast, the visible watermark is a user toggle outside India, South Korea and Vietnam, while the invisible SynthID mark always stays ([Flow Help: get started](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353333?hl=en)).

The terms also take a lot. Kling, Vidu and Dreamina say user content may be used to train their models. Vidu's licence is "perpetual, irrevocable" and covers commercial exploitation ([Vidu Terms](https://www.vidu.com/terms)). ElevenLabs takes a perpetual, sub-licensable licence over inputs and outputs ([ElevenLabs Terms](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). Unreleased designs or client material should therefore never be uploaded as references. On fal, model pages carry a "Commercial use" badge, and models marked "Research only" are non-commercial ([fal FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/faq.md)). The creator's own Remotion account deserves a check too. The free licence covers any individual and companies of up to three people, commercial use included ([Remotion licence FAQ](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/faq)). A paid seat ($25 a month) is needed only if the channel belongs to an organisation of four or more, for example an employer. Unreleased version 5.0 terms will also count contractors toward that headcount ([Remotion terms v5.0](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/terms)).

### Labels depend on realism, and copyright depends on human work

Mandatory AI labels apply to realistic content, not to "AI" as such. YouTube exempts non-realistic animation ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). TikTok requires labels for "realistic-looking scenes or people" ([TikTok Community Guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)). Meta's rule targets photorealistic video and realistic audio ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/governance/tracking-impact/labeling-ai-content/)). X's "Made with AI" toggle (`made_with_ai` in the API) is voluntary except for AI videos of armed conflict from paid creators ([X developer docs: create post](https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/create-post)). In the EU, Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026. A creator who earns "an economic benefit on a regular basis" counts as a deployer and must visibly label deepfakes. A hobbyist is outside the Act, and a platform's invisible marks never discharge the creator's own duty ([Commission Q&A](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act)). Free EU "AI" icons exist for the purpose ([EU icons page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-icons-labelling-ai-generated-content)). Platforms also auto-label from C2PA metadata, and YouTube says such labels "can not be adjusted" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). Gemini app output carries C2PA ([Gemini watermark settings](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17405358?hl=en)). Labels have a price: in a Copenhagen Business School experiment, an "AI-generated" label cut emotional engagement sharply on emotional posts, with a smaller cost on rational ones ([CBS](https://www.cbs.dk/en/articles/ai-labels-reduce-social-media-engagement)). Explainers and playful craft pieces are therefore the safer formats.

On copyright, the US Copyright Office says prompts alone are not authorship and that "repeatedly revising prompts does not change this analysis". Human selection and arrangement, modifications, and the creator's own work perceptible in the output are protectable ([Copyright Office Part 2 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). The Supreme Court declined to hear *Thaler* on 2 March 2026 ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-authorship-case)). A bare AI clip can therefore be lifted with little recourse. The edited video, script, narration, Remotion graphics and CadQuery models belong to the creator. Using an original character described in neutral visual terms, rather than "Pixar style" or a recognisable mascot, avoids the documented risk: YouTube removed dozens of AI Disney-character videos after a Disney letter ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/google-pulls-ai-generated-videos-of-disney-characters-from-youtube-in-response-to-cease-and-desist-220849629.html)).

### Monetisation needs subscriptions, thresholds and visible authorship

X retired revenue sharing on 7 September 2026. Its replacement, Original Content Rewards, requires an active paid subscription, 500 verified followers and 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in 90 days, and excludes content "created or posted using automated means" without saying how AI video is treated ([X Help: Original Content Rewards](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)). Whether the $8 Plus tier satisfies the subscription condition is likely but unconfirmed. YouTube excludes "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" while allowing a recurring character with a distinct idea per video. From 1 February 2027, Shorts ad revenue also needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days ([YouTube monetisation policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [YouTube Shorts monetisation](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)). TikTok's Creator Rewards pays only on original videos longer than one minute, which multiplies generation cost per post ([Gemlist](https://www.gemlist.io/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-requirements)). A cost-recovery plan should not count on platform payouts in the first year.

### Production details: consistency, audio, editing, upscaling and specifications

Character consistency comes from the image step. A character sheet, reference images restated in every prompt, and a storyboard grid split into three-panel strips are the documented techniques ([invideo FAQ](https://invideo.io/faq/why-use-a-3-panel-grid-instead-of-a-9-panel-grid-when/)). Across shots, the best-supported video models are the H3 family and Seedance 2.5 on Artificial Analysis's multi-scene and spatio-temporal categories, with Veo 3.1 Lite the weakest of the cheap options ([AA image-to-video leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Native audio is fine for a single clip, but each generated clip brings its own room tone, so a cut needs one music bed and one voice. Music taken from inside TikTok or Instagram is licensed for that platform only and cannot follow the clip to X ([Third Chair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/tiktok-commercial-music-library-what-it-covers-and-what-it-doesn-t)). That makes music generated under the creator's paid ElevenLabs plan, which includes music commercial use, the clean cross-platform choice.

No editor purchase is needed. Remotion is the editor here, and Clipchamp's free plan exports clean 1080p if a timeline tool is ever wanted ([Fluxnote on Clipchamp](https://fluxnote.io/guides/clipchamp-free-plan-limitations)). CapCut's free tier watermarks any project that uses a Pro asset ([CapCut help](https://www.capcut.com/help/join-capcut-pro-for-exporting)). Upscaling a 768p clip to 1080p through a cloud upscaler costs cents, about $0.007 to $0.02 per second ([Muapi](https://muapi.ai/video-upscaler)). It is wasted on a free X account, which plays back at 720p ([X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices)), and paying for "2K" on H3 buys an upscale, not more detail ([fal MiniMax H3](https://fal.ai/models/minimax/h3/image-to-video)). One master works on all four platforms: **1080×1920, H.264 High Profile MP4, 30 fps, about 8 Mbps, AAC at 128 kbps or more**. Under 3 minutes keeps Reels and Shorts recommendable. X's developer documentation now allows 20 minutes and 8 GB for free accounts ([X developer docs](https://docs.x.com/x-api/media/quickstart/best-practices); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/?p=105118)). Clips should be uploaded natively, since X's public ranking code models watch and dwell signals on native media ([xai-org/x-algorithm](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)).

### Hidden costs and the learning curve

The largest hidden cost is retries. Vendors bill completed but unusable takes in full, so cost per usable shot runs 3 to 5 times the sticker price, and Higgsfield's own planning range is "three to five attempts" ([Higgsfield credits vs unlimited](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/credits-vs-unlimited-ai-video-generation)). The others are monthly credits that expire unused (Krea, Higgsfield, Kling, Flow), refunds that vanish once one credit is spent (Higgsfield: 7 days; Magnific: 30 days), constant promotions that hide list prices, VAT of about 20% on EU subscriptions quoted "excluding VAT", and resellers with thin track records. kie.ai has about 2.1 of 5 from 16 Trustpilot reviews and no status page, so balances there should stay small ([gengrades](https://gengrades.com/kie-ai)). Model retirements break workflows mid-month, as Flow's 5 December 2026 cut shows. The learning curve favours this creator: Flow takes minutes, the fal Playground about an hour, and Remotion and Blender are already known. The new skill to build is shot design for image-to-video: one subject, one camera move, and a still with a reason to move.

## Conclusion

The lesson of 2026 is that quality has stopped being expensive while consistency and authorship have not. An open-weights base model and its derivatives now fill most of the top ten image-to-video places for a few cents a second, so the differentiator is no longer which generator a creator pays for. It is the layer that no generator supplies: exact text and labels, a recurring look, one coherent soundtrack and a recognisable point of view. That layer is exactly what Remotion, ElevenLabs and the creator's engineering models already provide. The efficient move is to treat AI video as cheap, disposable footage feeding a reusable template, not as the product.

For the engineering channel the same logic points the other way. Its competitive advantage is that every frame is provably right, which is also what makes it copyright-protected, label-free and hard to imitate with "AI slop". The fastest gains there come from mining existing renders and moving simple motion into frame-driven code, not from generators. Before spending anything, the first week should be a test: three identical stills run through H3, HeyGen Video 1 and Seedance 2.5 on a $10 fal balance, one Veo 3.1 Lite run on Flow's free credits, and one `npx remotion gpu --gl=angle` check. Those inexpensive tests will settle the remaining unknowns better than any leaderboard: how each model handles this creator's own look, and how fast this particular PC renders 3D.
